# Country Market Guides — Priority Halal Markets

> Research ticket output for HalalNeo Phase 1. Eight priority markets profiled: Indonesia, Malaysia, Saudi Arabia, UAE, Turkey, Pakistan, Singapore, UK. Focus: market size, certification bodies, import requirements, mutual-recognition landscape, B2B buying characteristics.

## Executive Summary

- **Global context.** Muslim consumer spending reached **US$2.6 trillion in 2024**, projected at **US$3.56 trillion by 2029** (DinarStandard, State of the Global Islamic Economy 2025/26). Halal food alone was **US$1.53 trillion in 2024**, heading to **US$2.06 trillion by 2029**. Separate market-research firms quote wildly different totals (US$0.9tn–2.2tn) because definitions differ; use DinarStandard/ICDT as the anchor figures.
- **Indonesia is the prize but the hardest entry.** It is the world's largest halal market (~277M Muslims, ~US$626B market size) and the largest halal food importer in the OIC (US$25.8B in 2024). From **17 Oct 2026**, mandatory halal certification expands to most imported food & beverage products. BPJPH (the sole authority) accepts foreign certificates **only via MRAs/LHLN registration**, and foreign certificates have shorter validity (~1 year) than domestic (4 years).
- **Three-tier recognition landscape.** (1) **Mandatory domestic authority + MRA list** (Indonesia/BPJPH, Malaysia/JAKIM, Singapore/MUIS, Saudi/SFDA–Saudi Halal Center, UAE/MOIAT): foreign halal certificates are accepted only from bodies each authority formally recognizes. (2) **Accreditation-gated** (Turkey/HAK): certificates accepted only if issued by a HAK-accredited body. (3) **Voluntary, unregulated** (UK, Pakistan domestic): no single state authority; exporters must chase certificates recognized by their _destination_ market.
- **GCC (Saudi + UAE) are high-value, regulated, and expanding fast**, driven by Vision 2030 giga-projects and Dubai's re-export model. Halal certification is legally mandatory for specific imported categories and is enforced at the border (SFDA halal shipment certificate; MOIAT-registered certifying bodies).
- **B2B buying is certification-first.** In every Muslim-majority market, a valid halal certificate from a recognized body is a precondition to even be listed by importers, distributors, and retail chains — not a differentiator. Ingredients, meat/poultry, and processed food are the main import segments; HORECA and retail chains are the anchor buyers.

## 1. Indonesia

**Market size / outlook.** Largest halal market in the world with ~277M Muslim consumers (91% of population). The government frames the opportunity as a **~US$626 billion market** it is leveraging to become a global halal hub (Jakarta Globe, Jun 2026). Indonesia was the **largest halal food importer in the OIC at US$25.82B in 2024** (ICDT), ahead of Malaysia. It imported **240,000+ tonnes of halal meat in 2023**, up from 210,000t in 2022. USDA notes mandatory halal certification will expand to most food & beverage products from **17 October 2026** — a major structural change for importers.

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** Halal Product Assurance Agency (**BPJPH**, under the Ministry of Religious Affairs) is the sole halal authority under Law UU 33/2014. It certifies through LPHs (halal inspection bodies, e.g. LPPOM MUI) and issues certificates backed by halal _fatwa_ (from MUI). BPJPH maintains the **LHLN list of overseas halal certification bodies**; it has signed **MRAs with 114+ foreign halal bodies** across dozens of countries (Emerhub, 2026).

**Import requirements.** Under UU 33/2014 halal certification is mandatory for food & beverages (phased: F&B from 17 Oct 2024; broader rollout 2026–2034). Imported products need: BPOM food registration, a halal certificate registered with BPJPH (via an MRA-covered LHLN or in-country certification), compliant labelling, and importer registration (API-U/API-P). Halal certificates are valid **4 years** domestically; BPJPH has proposed limiting **foreign certificates to 1 year**.

**Mutual recognition.** Foreign halal certificates are **accepted only if the issuing body has an MRA with BPJPH and the certificate is registered** in the BPJPH system. Bodies without an MRA (or products outside MRA scope) must be re-certified in-country. A new **SJPH self-declaration** scheme and updated import compliance procedures are under consultation (2026). Practical reality: check the LHLN list before engaging a foreign certifier.

**B2B buying characteristics.** Importers/distributors will not buy without a valid, BPJPH-recognized halal certificate — it is a listing precondition. Main demand: **food-processing ingredients**, meat, dairy, and packaged processed foods. Distribution is through registered national importers and wholesalers feeding modern retail, food service (the "food court" culture is huge), and food manufacturing. Certification reliability is a core due-diligence criterion for Indonesian buyers.

## 2. Malaysia

**Market size / outlook.** A leading halal _hub_ and gateway. Halal food imports were valued at **US$20.8 billion in 2024 (≈7% of total imports)** (HKTDC). MATRADE projects the halal market to reach **RM523.53B (≈US$113.2B) by 2030**. The halal economy contributes ~7.5% of GDP (World Bank). Malaysia consistently ranks top in the Global Islamic Economy Index for food and is a major halal exporter and re-export gateway into ASEAN.

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** **JAKIM** (Department of Islamic Development Malaysia) is the sole national halal certification authority (with state MUIS/MUIS branches). JAKIM runs **HALMAS** and issues Malaysia's halal logo. For imports, JAKIM maintains a published list of **Foreign Halal Certification Bodies (FHCB)** it recognizes (e.g. AHF).

**Import requirements.** All imported **animal products except pork must be halal**; meat and meat-based products (including poultry) **must carry halal certification from a JAKIM-recognized FHCB**, and meat/poultry consignments are also inspected and approved by the **Department of Veterinary Services (DVS)**. The Trade Descriptions Act/Order prevents imported goods from being described as halal without certification. Labelling and food safety clearance fall under the Ministry of Health (FoSIM).

**Mutual recognition.** Foreign halal certificates are **accepted for imports only from JAKIM-recognized FHCBs**; JAKIM re-audits/recognizes the foreign body, not just the certificate. JAKIM has also signed MoUs/MRAs with other national authorities (e.g. SFDA, BPJPH) and co-founded the **International Halal Integrity Alliance (IHI)**.

**B2B buying characteristics.** Malaysia's value proposition is as a **re-export and value-add gateway** into Indonesia, China, and the Middle East. Buyers include halal food manufacturers, distributors, and retailers operating under the HALMAS ecosystem. Strong halal logistics (halal-certified cold chain/warehousing) is a buying criterion. HDC (Halal Development Corporation) supports foreign investment into halal manufacturing for export.

## 3. Saudi Arabia

**Market size / outlook.** Saudi halal food demand is estimated around **US$5 billion annually**, and KSA is the **second-largest GCC-6 market for imported processed food after the UAE** (Food Export, halalrc). Growth is powered by **Vision 2030**, giga-projects (NEOM, Red Sea), tourism targets, and a young, brand-literate population. The food certification services market is expanding as SFDA tightens halal rules (TechSci, 2025).

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** **SFDA (Saudi Food and Drug Authority)** regulates food imports. Its **Saudi Halal Center (SHC)** issues halal certification and maintains a **designated/recognized list of foreign certification bodies** (halal.gov.sa). SFDA applies **GSO 2055-1 / GSO 993** halal standards and has signed halal mutual-recognition MoUs (e.g. with JAKIM).

**Import requirements.** Foreign food establishments and importers must **register with SFDA (FASEH e-account)**; items are registered per SKU. Required docs include: certified invoice, **certificate of origin, original halal certificate, and slaughtering certificate for meat/poultry**. Since **March 2025**, SFDA made halal certification **mandatory for five imported food categories**, and a **Halal Shipment Certificate** is required for food exports to the Kingdom. Consignments are checked at border inspection points (BIPs) — documentary, identity, physical, and lab checks; rejection risk is real.

**Mutual recognition.** Foreign halal certificates are **accepted only from bodies recognized by SFDA/Saudi Halal Center**. The center operates a formal **designation/recognition process** for foreign CBs and holds bilateral MoUs with other national authorities. Verify recognition on the SHC list before exporting.

**B2B buying characteristics.** Most major Saudi importers operate **well-established distribution networks selling direct to retailers, wholesalers, and the HRI sector** (USDA Retail Foods). Buyers are volume-oriented, quality-sensitive, and favour established brands with ironclad halal assurance plus HACCP/ISO credentials. HORECA and giga-project catering are fast-growing channels; e-grocery is rising.

## 4. UAE

**Market size / outlook.** The UAE halal food & beverage market is the **third largest globally** and the country is a global re-export hub. It **imports ~US$20 billion of halal food annually**, of which **unpackaged meat is ~80%**; retail is ~44% of sales (Farrelly Mitchell). The halal food & lifestyle economy is valued around **US$25B (2025)** and projected to grow at ~8.75% CAGR (Research&Markets, Ken Research).

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** **MOIAT** (Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology) is the national halal authority under **Cabinet Decree No. 10 of 2014**. It maintains a **Registered Halal Certification Bodies** list and issues the optional **Halal National Mark**. Standards: **UAE.S 2055-1/2, UAE.S 993** (slaughtering), aligned to GSO. Accreditation flows through **ENAS/EIAC** and international bodies.

**Import requirements.** Food importers must be licensed by the relevant municipality/emirate (e.g. Dubai Municipality under **EHS/Foodwatch**) and comply with MOIAT's halal regime. Products containing or derived from animal sources require a **halal certificate issued by a MOIAT-registered halal certification body**; certificates must be traceable to an **ENAS-accredited CB** or a recognized accreditation body (eurosaleonline, 2026). Documents must be in English/multilingual. Dubai's FoodWatch system is used for registration.

**Mutual recognition.** MOIAT **registers foreign halal certification bodies** — their certificates are accepted at import for the registered scope. The Halal National Mark is optional but eases movement of halal products into and through the UAE. The UAE also promotes mutual recognition bilaterally and via GSO.

**B2B buying characteristics.** UAE buyers operate as **importers, re-exporters, and distributors for retail chains and HORECA**; the country is a springboard into the wider MENA market. Free zones (JAFZA, DMCC) are the logistics engine. Price competition is intense because re-export margins are thin; halal assurance, cold-chain integrity, and consistent supply win contracts.

## 5. Turkey

**Market size / outlook.** Turkey's halal food market was valued at **US$38.95B in 2025**, projected to reach **US$103.98B by 2034** (~10% CAGR, IMARC). Turkey is a large producer/exporter rather than a pure importer — halal is an **export driver** to OIC and GCC markets. Halal certification is **voluntary** for domestic sales.

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** The **Halal Accreditation Agency (HAK / Helal Akreditasyon Kurumu)** is the **only authorized institution to accredit halal conformity assessment bodies** in Turkey (Law 7011). It accredits bodies like **GIMDES, TSE**, and foreign CBs. HAK accredits foreign certification bodies too, extending Turkish accreditation to overseas certifiers.

**Import requirements.** There is no blanket mandatory halal certification for imports; but since 2024, **products carrying halal claims** entering the Turkish market must be certified by **HAK-accredited certification bodies**, and claims are verified through Turkey's **electronic Single Window system** (raqam). General food imports follow Turkish Food Codex + Ministry of Agriculture rules; halal is a claim-level requirement, not a blanket one.

**Mutual recognition.** Acceptance is **accreditation-gated, not MRA-gated**: a foreign halal certificate is recognized if its issuer holds HAK accreditation. HAK actively accredits foreign CBs (e.g. Al-Baqara, China). This is a lighter-touch model than BPJPH/JAKIM but still formal.

**B2B buying characteristics.** Buyers are predominantly **food processors and exporters** seeking halal-certified inputs and re-export opportunities into MENA/EU markets. Istanbul hosts major halal trade platforms (World Halal Summit). For foreign suppliers, the practical entry is via HAK-accredited certification and partnerships with Turkish importers/food manufacturers.

## 6. Pakistan

**Market size / outlook.** Pakistan is the **world's second-largest Muslim-majority population** (~230M+), giving a huge domestic halal market; but it captures only **~0.26% of the global halal market**, concentrated in meat (scribd/policy analyses). Halal **meat exports are the flagship opportunity** — e.g. a five-year deal to export Rs 47B (~130,000t) of halal meat to Malaysia (2025). Research (NIPAS) emphasizes under-exploited potential in global halal meat trade.

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** The **Pakistan Halal Authority (PHA)** is the federal halal regulator and certifies products/facilities (halal status + accreditation of halal certification bodies). **PSQCA** (Pakistan Standards and Quality Control Authority) develops the halal standards (**PS 3733:2010** general requirements for halal food; **PS 4992:2010** halal certification criteria). Private bodies and exporters are PHA/PSQCA-driven.

**Import requirements.** Halal certification is **required for food imports**, especially **animal products** (meat & poultry mandatory halal). Labelling must be in English or Urdu; imports must meet PSQCA standards. A halal certificate from a recognized body is the norm for animal-origin imports (USDA/PSQCA guidance).

**Mutual recognition.** No comprehensive national MRA network like JAKIM/BPJPH. Acceptance of foreign halal certificates is **body-by-body and often destination-driven**: for exports, Pakistani exporters must match the _destination_ country's recognition (e.g. JAKIM-recognized bodies for Malaysia, SFDA-recognized for Saudi). For imports into Pakistan, PHA/PSQCA recognition applies in practice.

**B2B buying characteristics.** The domestic opportunity is **import substitution and ingredients** for a large food-processing and agro-industrial base; the export opportunity is **halal meat and value-added foods** into the Gulf, Malaysia, and the wider OIC. Buyers in Pakistan (processors, retailers, restaurant chains) increasingly demand certified halal inputs; price sensitivity is high.

## 7. Singapore

**Market size / outlook.** Singapore's halal food industry is estimated around **US$1 billion** (2019 estimate, Frontiers), but its strategic value is as **the world's largest food re-export hub** for halal products into ASEAN (Malaysia, Indonesia). ~15% of residents are Muslim, so domestic demand is concentrated but sophisticated.

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** **MUIS** (Islamic Religious Council of Singapore) is the **sole halal certification authority**. MUIS certifies local establishments and runs the **Foreign Halal Certification Bodies (FHCB) recognition scheme** for imports, aligned to the Singapore MUIS Halal Standards (SMHS). MUIS does not certify products made overseas — it recognizes the overseas body instead.

**Import requirements.** All commercial food imports require **SFA registration and import permits via TradeNet**. Halal-labelled imports must be certified by a **MUIS-recognized FHCB** from the country of origin. From **1 October 2025**, MUIS enhanced the FHCB framework: online application, risk-tiered evaluation (Tier 1/2/3), transparent fees (S$1,000 application; S$2,000 recognition; S$700/day audit), **3-year recognition** with annual reporting.

**Mutual recognition.** Acceptance is **entirely through MUIS's FHCB recognition list**. A foreign certificate is only valid for import if the issuing body is currently MUIS-recognized for the relevant scope. No blanket MRAs; each body is assessed against SMHS.

**B2B buying characteristics.** Buyers are **re-exporters, food manufacturers, and food-service/retail chains** operating with tight halal-assurance and traceability requirements. Reliability, supply consistency, and halal integrity matter more than price. Singapore is also a **testing ground and premium market** for halal innovation (cultured meat debates, new food tech).

## 8. United Kingdom

**Market size / outlook.** The UK halal market is large but the figures vary wildly by source: AHDB estimates UK Muslims (~6.5% of population) spent **£4.64 billion on halal food & beverages** (2022–2024 consumer insight), with halal retail sales exceeding £2.5B (Market Data Forecast). Syndicated market-research firms publish much higher "UK halal food market" figures (US$89–97B, Research&Markets/IMARC) — treat these as broad economy-wide estimates, not retail food spend. Growth follows the young, growing British Muslim demographic and halal's mainstreaming in retail and foodservice.

**Recognised certifying bodies / accreditation.** There is **no single government halal authority** in the UK. **~20 private halal certification bodies** operate (HFA, Halal Monitoring Committee, Halal Food Council, and others) under general food law (FSA/GFS). Certificates from these bodies are **not legally regulated or mutually recognized by UK authorities** — recognition matters only in _export_ markets.

**Import requirements.** The UK itself does not mandate halal certification for imports; general UK food law applies (FSA, labelling, hygiene). The critical halal requirement is **outward-facing**: UK exports must be certified by bodies recognized by the destination country (e.g. JAKIM-recognized for Malaysia, SFDA-recognized for Saudi) or the export fails. AHDB flags that certificates issued by UK bodies are **often not recognized by foreign authorities** — a real friction point for exporters.

**Mutual recognition.** No formal framework. Halal assurance in the UK is **market-driven**: retail chains and foodservice operators impose their own supplier assurance standards (e.g. slaughterhouse audits), and exporters must stack destination-market recognition on top.

**B2B buying characteristics.** Anchor buyers are **national retailers** (halal ranges at Tesco, Asda, Sainsbury's, Morrisons), **foodservice** (chicken shops, takeaways, catering), and **wholesale distributors**. Buying is dominated by poultry and red meat with a strong emphasis on certified slaughter and supply-chain assurance. For foreign suppliers, the UK works best as a **volume market and a staging point for EU/OIC export**.
