# Halal certification landscape
Type: research
Status: resolved
Blocked by: none

## Question

Research the global halal certification landscape to feed the knowledge base's **Halal Certification** section and the certification-status fields on Merchant/SKU pages.

- What are the mainstream certifying bodies (e.g. MUI Indonesia, JAKIM Malaysia, GAC Saudi Arabia, SFDA, MUIS Singapore, KMF, etc.) and where are they recognised?
- What are the main halal standards/regimes (e.g. OIC/SMIIC, GSO, national standards) and how do they differ?
- What does a typical halal certification process look like (application → audit → issuance → renewal), and what are typical costs and timelines?
- What triggers/requirements matter most to B2B buyers evaluating a supplier's certification?

Capture findings as `## Answer` below, with sources.

## Answer

Full findings in [research/01-halal-certification.md](../research/01-halal-certification.md). Gist: a patchwork regime — no single halal standard; each importing authority accepts certs from its own recognition list. Dominant trio: JAKIM (MS 1500), MUI/BPJPH (HAS 23000), MUIS (SS 590), plus Gulf GSO 2055 (GAC/SFDA, MOIAT). Standards differ on stunning/slaughter/seafood — a certificate doesn't transfer across markets. Process: apply → doc review → audit + lab → Shariah review → issue → renew; ~4-7 weeks, $250–$7k/yr. Buyers care about: scope, recognition in target market, expiry, audit history, verifiable cert numbers.
